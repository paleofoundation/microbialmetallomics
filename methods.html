Methods & Instruments

You can't manage what you can't measure.

Microbial metallomics is defined by its instruments. Four questions organize the whole toolbox: how much metal is present, where it sits, how it's bound, and which protein carries it. Each has its own technique.

Start with the question, then choose the measurement.

A metal concentration, an elemental map, and a metal-binding protein answer different questions. Use this comparison to see what each measurement can establish and what needs a second method.

Compare all four research questions below.

How much metal is present?

Start with
ICP-MS for total elemental content.
What it tells you
The amount of each measured element in the sample; it can also support metal-to-protein stoichiometry for a characterized protein sample.
Interpretation limit
A total does not reveal where the metal sits or whether it belongs to a cytochrome, a storage protein, or another pool.
To answer the next question
Add elemental imaging for location, XAS for the chemical environment, or metalloproteomics to investigate protein association.

Where is the metal?

Compare
LA-ICP-MS imaging maps material removed by a scanning laser. Synchrotron X-ray fluorescence maps elemental fluorescence in cells or tissues.
What it tells you
How an element is distributed across the region being imaged, making spatial patterns visible that a whole-sample total cannot show.
Interpretation limit
An elemental map alone does not identify the protein carrying the metal or establish its oxidation state.
To answer the next question
Use XAS to investigate the chemical environment or metalloproteomics for protein-associated metal fractions.

What is its chemical environment?

Start with
X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS) to investigate chemical speciation through oxidation state and local coordination.
What it tells you
XANES probes oxidation state; EXAFS provides information about the metal's neighboring atoms, coordination, and bond distances.
Interpretation limit
Knowing that copper has a particular oxidation state and sulfur ligands does not, by itself, identify which protein it belongs to.
To answer the next question
Combine the chemical picture with native MS or metalloproteomics when the question concerns a metal–protein complex.

Which proteins carry metal?

Compare
Native MS examines intact metal–protein complexes. SEC-ICP-MS separates a sample into fractions and detects their metal content.
What it tells you
Native MS can reveal metal occupancy and complex assembly. Metalloproteomics connects metal measurements with the proteins in separated fractions.
Interpretation limit
Protein association is a different question from the metal's distribution in a cell or tissue, and from the detailed chemistry of its binding site.
To answer the next question
Add XAS for local binding chemistry or elemental imaging for spatial context. Sequence-based prediction can help frame candidates before measurement.

Use this as a guide to interpreting evidence. The technique descriptions and their sources are below; no single measurement reconstructs the entire metallome.

HOW MUCH

ICP-MS

Inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry is the quantitative backbone of the field. It vaporizes a sample in an argon plasma and counts atoms of each element down to parts-per-trillion — giving total metal content and the precise stoichiometry of a metalloprotein.

WHERE

LA-ICP-MS imaging

Laser-ablation ICP-MS rasters a focused laser across a tissue section or single cell, feeding each ablated spot into the mass spectrometer to build a pixel-by-pixel elemental map. It reveals metal distribution — now approaching single-cell resolution.

WHERE

Synchrotron X-ray fluorescence

Focused synchrotron X-rays excite metals to fluoresce, producing highly sensitive elemental maps of intact cells and tissues in situ. Dedicated beamlines — Diamond's I18 and I14, ESRF's ID21, the Australian Synchrotron's XFM — reach sub-micron and nanometer resolution.

HOW BOUND

X-ray absorption spectroscopy

XAS is non-destructive and metal-specific. Its XANES region reports a metal's oxidation state; its EXAFS region reveals coordination geometry, ligand identity, and bond distances. The high-resolution HERFD-XAS variant sharpens spectral features for finer electronic detail — the closest thing to a photograph of a metal site.

WHICH PROTEIN

Native mass spectrometry

By keeping proteins folded and intact in the gas phase, native MS preserves fragile metal–protein complexes — measuring how many metal ions a protein carries and the assembly state of a metalloprotein without tearing the metal off.

WHICH PROTEIN

Metalloproteomics (SEC-ICP-MS)

Coupling metal-compatible liquid chromatography to ICP-MS separates a whole proteome and detects which fractions carry metal — linking a specific metal to the protein that holds it. Multidimensional HPLC-ICP-MS now scales this into a "metalloproteome factory."

And before the bench: prediction

Sequence- and structure-based tools such as mebipred predict metal-binding sites across whole genomes, estimating the size and identity of a species' metalloproteome before a single experiment is run — the computational front end of modern metallomics.

Why it takes a toolbox

No single instrument answers every question. ICP-MS tells you a cell holds, say, ten million iron atoms — but not whether they're in cytochromes or stuck in a storage protein. XAS tells you a copper is in the +1 state with two sulfur ligands — but not which protein it belongs to. Only by combining elemental quantification, spatial imaging, structural spectroscopy, and metalloproteomics does the full metallome come into focus. That integration is what makes metallomics an "-omics" rather than a collection of assays.

Official beamline guides describe the capabilities of Diamond I14[3] and I18[4], ESRF ID21[5], and the Australian Synchrotron XFM beamline[6].
