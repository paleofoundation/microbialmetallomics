Historical profile

Hiroki Haraguchi

Pioneer of metallomics

Nagoya University · Nagoya, Japan

Proposed metallomics as an integrated field for studying metals and metal-containing biomolecules. His foundational 2004 paper connects elemental analysis and chemical speciation with the questions raised by genomics and proteomics.

  • Metallomics
  • Elemental analysis
  • Speciation

Historical profile

Robert J. P. Williams †

Emeritus Professor of Chemistry

University of Oxford · Oxford, UK

Helped establish bioinorganic chemistry and the Irving–Williams stability series. His work connected metal coordination, enzyme function and biological evolution, providing a chemical foundation for understanding why cells use particular elements.

  • Coordination chemistry
  • Metalloenzymes
  • Evolution

Barry P. Rosen

Distinguished University Professor Emeritus

Florida International University · Miami, USA

Studies how organisms transport, transform and detoxify arsenic and other metals. His work on arsenic uptake, efflux, biomethylation and organoarsenical chemistry links bacterial resistance mechanisms to environmental exposure. FIU records his retirement in 2025.

  • Arsenic
  • Detoxification
  • Transport

Site author

Karen Pendergrass

ORCID 0000-0002-2348-7259

Microbial Metallomics Researcher & Founder

Microbiome Medicine · Heavy Metal Index · Wikibiome · Institute of Contaminant Standards (ICS)

Karen Pendergrass investigates upstream metal contamination in food systems and agricultural inputs, and how these exposures may influence microbial metabolism and disease. Her work connects exposure pathways with mismetallation and metal-dependent enzyme function, with particular attention to nickel, urease, [NiFe]-hydrogenases, and nickel-dependent forms of glyoxalase I. By examining microbiome and metallomic signatures together, she explores how changes in metal availability and enzyme activity may link microbial communities and heavy-metal exposure to conditions such as endometriosis and Parkinson’s disease.

She is the founder of Microbiome Medicine, Heavy Metal Index, Wikibiome, and the Institute of Contaminant Standards (ICS).

  • Food & agricultural contamination
  • Mismetallation
  • Nickel & microbial enzymes
  • Microbiome & metallomic signatures
  • Endometriosis
  • Parkinson’s disease
Institutional profile ↗karen@paleofoundation.com

Research profile

Divine Aleru

ORCID 0009-0008-1157-6327

Microbiome Research Curator & Medical Writer

Microbiome Medicine (2025–2026) · Bioconductor / BugSigDB (Outreachy)

Divine Aleru translates microbiome research into structured evidence and accessible scientific writing. Her work centers on the interpretation of microbial signatures, study design and disease context, helping readers compare findings across studies and understand what the evidence can support.

As Microbiome Research Coordinator at Microbiome Medicine (2025–2026), she developed and published more than 300 research pages across 15 disease areas, critically appraised clinical and microbiome studies, and wrote research reviews for specialist and non-specialist audiences.

Through Bioconductor’s Outreachy program, she curated more than 100 peer-reviewed studies for BugSigDB, reviewed over 200 studies to improve curation accuracy, and contributed to R tools for analyzing and reporting microbial-signature data. She subsequently served as an Outreachy co-mentor. Her laboratory experience at the University of Chester includes mammalian cell culture and Human Tissue Act training.

  • Microbial signatures
  • Evidence synthesis
  • Disease-associated microbiomes
  • Medical writing
  • Open science
