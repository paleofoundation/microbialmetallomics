Mechanism & significance

What changes in the microbe, and why

  • Two necessary proteins. Deleting hgcA or hgcB abolished mercury methylation in the two tested anaerobic bacteria. HgcA was identified as a corrinoid-protein candidate and HgcB as an iron–sulfur ferredoxin, connecting the conversion to methyl-transfer and electron-transfer machinery. [1]
  • Where the methyl group comes from. Zheng and colleagues purified the membrane-associated HgcAB complex and demonstrated transfer from S-adenosyl-L-methionine, or SAM, to its cobalamin center. HgcB contains two [4Fe–4S] clusters. Cell-lysate methylmercury assays and purified-protein measurements identified SAM as the methyl donor; the purification detergent impaired the mercury-transfer half-reaction. This resolves a chemical step without presenting the entire catalytic cycle as fully reconstituted. [2]
  • A survey of more than 3,500 metagenomes found hgcAB across many anaerobic environments and additional microbial lineages. The genes were effectively absent from roughly 1,500 human and other mammalian microbiomes in that dataset. This was evidence about genetic potential, not a direct measurement of methylation rates. [3]

The broader picture

Microbes change mercury’s biological consequences by changing its molecular form. HgcAB-dependent methylation converts inorganic mercury into methylmercury, using a cobalt-containing cobalamin cofactor and iron–sulfur chemistry. Intestinal demethylation is a separate process that can contribute to elimination after ingestion. The central question is which chemical conversion is occurring, where, and at what rate—not simply whether mercury and microbes coexist. [1][4][2]

Experiments worth knowing

Two anaerobic bacterial species

A genetic test of methylmercury production

Study design. Targeted deletions of hgcA and hgcB.

What they found. Deleting either gene eliminated measured methylation in both organisms. [1]

How to read it. This supports a necessary role in the tested strains; gene abundance remains distinct from reaction rate in nature.

Public environmental and host-associated metagenomes

Mapping methylation potential

Study design. Comparative searches for conserved hgcAB genes across thousands of datasets.

What they found. Potential methylators occurred in diverse anaerobic habitats, with little evidence of the genes in the surveyed mammalian microbiomes. [3]

How to read it. Environmental mercury methylation should not be assumed to be a normal human gut function.

Community & host evidence

A coordinated study of 27 people and gnotobiotic mice connected stool demethylation activity with methylmercury elimination. Human half-lives varied widely. Human stool transplants restored elimination in germ-free mice, whereas an associated bacterial species alone did not. The findings point to community and host contributions rather than a single universally protective organism. [4]

Exposure & chemical form

Methylmercury ingested with fish is chemically and biologically distinct from inorganic Hg(II) or elemental Hg(0). Genetic surveys are useful for locating potential methylators in the environment, but actual production also requires an accessible mercury substrate and conditions that support the reaction. [1][3]

What the evidence does not establish

A positive hgcAB sequence does not quantify environmental methylmercury production. Conversely, its absence from a dataset is not proof of universal absence from every gut. The human study found mixed effects of a prebiotic, and metagenomic analysis did not identify the conventional merB demethylation pathway. [4][3]

Open research questions

Questions suggested by the evidence and its limits.

  • Which gut microbial enzymes support demethylation when the conventional merB pathway is not detected? [4]
  • Which chemical and ecological measurements best predict realized methylation from an hgcAB-positive community? [1][3]
