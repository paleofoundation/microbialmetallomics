Why it belongs in microbial metallomics

Magnesium is a major cellular ion that links ribosome stability, energy metabolism, and environmental sensing. Bacteria can monitor magnesium outside the cell through proteins and inside the cell through RNA-based systems. These separate control points help explain why total magnesium is a poor substitute for knowing the available pool at a particular molecular target. [1][2][3]

What microbes do with it

  • An extracellular signal. In Salmonella, magnesium changed the conformation of the periplasmic sensor domain of PhoQ and regulated PhoP-dependent genes. Mutating the sensor changed its response threshold and affected virulence. Magnesium therefore acts both as cellular material and as information about the environment the bacterium occupies. [1]
  • Ribosomes connect magnesium to phosphate. Low cytoplasmic magnesium destabilized Salmonella ribosomes and slowed protein synthesis. Reduced translation lowered ATP consumption, trapping phosphate in ATP and triggering a phosphate-starvation response despite plentiful external phosphate. Restoration of magnesium balance and ATP consumption ended that response. Apparent starvation can therefore emerge from altered intracellular allocation. [2]
  • RNA can sense the ion directly. The M-box riboswitch adopts a compact structure in response to divalent metal binding and regulates genes involved in magnesium homeostasis. Structural, genetic, and biochemical experiments established a metal-sensing role for RNA itself, adding a regulatory route alongside protein sensors such as PhoQ. [3]

Experiments worth knowing

Salmonella, with complementary experiments in E. coli and yeast

Protein synthesis changes the meaning of nutrient scarcity

Study design. In vitro genetics and physiology.

What they found. Impaired translation elicited phosphate-starvation signaling even when external phosphate was available. [2]

How to read it. Magnesium stress can propagate through translation and ATP turnover to phosphate regulation.

M-box regulatory RNA

A metal sensor made of RNA

Study design. In vitro structural and biochemical analysis combined with bacterial genetics.

What they found. Magnesium-dependent RNA folding controlled accessibility of regulatory sequences. [3]

How to read it. Metal homeostasis can be regulated through RNA architecture as well as metal-binding proteins.

Community & host evidence

The experiments here identify mechanisms in model bacteria, not a predictable community response to magnesium supplementation. They distinguish extracellular sensing, intracellular availability, and downstream metabolic consequences. A community can contain organisms using different sensors and uptake systems; a shared external magnesium concentration need not place them in the same physiological state. [1][2][3]

Exposure & chemical form

Magnesium added to culture medium, magnesium bound to RNA, and free cytoplasmic magnesium are separate quantities. ATP and ribosomes participate in the intracellular balance. A transcriptomic phosphate-starvation signature, for example, need not mean that environmental phosphate is scarce if translation and ATP turnover have changed. [2]

What the evidence does not establish

PhoQ findings should not be generalized to organisms that lack the same regulatory system. M-box distribution suggests a broader RNA-based strategy but does not establish identical thresholds or target genes in every lineage. These studies also do not show that host blood magnesium reports the availability encountered by gut bacteria. [1][3]

Open research questions

Questions suggested by the evidence and its limits.

  • How do RNA sensors and protein sensors coordinate when external magnesium and the free cytoplasmic pool give different signals? [1][3]
  • Can paired measurements of translation, ATP, and free magnesium resolve misleading nutrient-starvation signatures in microbial communities? [2]
