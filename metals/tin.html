Why it belongs in microbial metallomics

Tin–microbe interactions are strongly compound dependent. Environmental bacteria can remove organic groups from tributyltin or add methyl groups to inorganic tin. Separately, organotin exposure can alter a mammalian gut community. Neither process can be generalized to all tin compounds, and transformation does not necessarily reduce toxicity. [1][2]

What microbes do with it

  • Isotope-tracing culture experiments showed tributyltin dealkylation by Desulfobulbus propionicus and selected Pseudomonas isolates. The same study detected methylation of inorganic tin and butyltin breakdown products. A single culture can therefore drive reactions in different directions within the tin-speciation network. [1]
  • A mouse transfer experiment moved fecal communities from highly tributyltin-exposed donors to recipients. Some changes in body weight, fat accumulation, glucose handling, and hormone measurements were reproduced, supporting a contribution from the transferred community in that experimental system. [2]

Experiments worth knowing

Pure environmental bacterial cultures

Following tin through competing reactions

Study design. Isotope-resolved measurements distinguished dealkylation from methylation.

What they found. Some isolates both degraded tributyltin and generated methylated tin compounds; tested Desulfovibrio strains did not transform tin. [1]

How to read it. Genus-level labels and disappearance of the starting compound are insufficient to establish complete removal.

ICR mice and fecal-community recipients

Testing a transferable phenotype

Study design. Eight weeks of oral tributyltin exposure followed by microbiota transplantation from the higher-dose group.

What they found. Recipients reproduced several metabolic and endocrine phenotypes of exposed donors. [2]

How to read it. This supports involvement of a transferable intestinal component in mice, not a proven human causal pathway.

Community & host evidence

In a separate early-life mouse experiment, intermittent tributyltin chloride exposure from postnatal days 24 to 54 was followed by altered bacterial diversity and composition and greater weight gain in males at day 84. Those associations motivated a microbiome hypothesis; they did not by themselves demonstrate mediation. [3]

Exposure & chemical form

Environmental culture work tested tributyltin and isotope-enriched inorganic tin under aerobic, nitrate-reducing, or sulfate-reducing conditions. The mouse studies administered an organotin compound. Inorganic tin, tin in an alloy, and tributyltin should not share a single exposure label or inferred gut effect. [1][3]

What the evidence does not establish

Transfer experiments strengthen causal inference but do not identify a particular bacterial species or exclude all material transferred with feces. Both mouse studies involve specified dosing regimens, not population-level human exposures. A phylum ratio or isolated taxon change is not a validated organotin biomarker. [2][3]

Open research questions

Questions suggested by the evidence and its limits.

  • Which organotin products accumulate during treatment, and does the final mixture become less toxic? [1]
  • Can defined microbial functions reproduce the transplantation result while excluding residual exposure chemicals? [2]
