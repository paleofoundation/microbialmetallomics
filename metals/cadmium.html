Cadmium & microbes

Toxicant with a specialized algal enzyme exception

Why it belongs in microbial metallomics

Cadmium is studied as a gut toxicant, but its biology is not uniformly nonfunctional: certain marine diatoms can use it in carbonic anhydrase under zinc limitation. [1][2]

What microbes do with it

  • Germ-free mice absorbed and accumulated more orally administered cadmium than conventional mice, implicating the microbiota and host intestinal metal-handling responses. [1]
  • Thalassiosira weissflogii produces a cadmium-containing carbonic anhydrase involved in inorganic-carbon acquisition. This narrow adaptation is not evidence that cadmium is a human nutrient. [2]

Community & host evidence

Oral cadmium exposure produced modest, taxon-specific shifts in a mouse sequencing study; effects should not be converted into a fixed human dysbiosis signature. [3]

Exposure & chemical form

The gut experiments used soluble cadmium salts by oral exposure. The diatom result concerns low-zinc marine culture conditions. [1][3][2]

What the evidence does not establish

Dose, metal availability, zinc status, and species determine the result. Mouse absorption data and marine algal biochemistry cannot establish clinical benefit or a safe dietary dose. [1][2]
