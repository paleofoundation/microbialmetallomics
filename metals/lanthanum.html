Mechanism & significance

What changes in the microbe, and why

  • A metal–cofactor partnership. ExaF purified from Methylobacterium extorquens AM1 preferentially contained La(III) even when calcium was available, together with PQQ, the redox cofactor. Enzyme assays connected that metal choice to ethanol oxidation and activity on other alcohol or aldehyde substrates. [1]
  • Why the metal’s chemistry matters. In related XoxF alcohol dehydrogenases, lanthanides act as Lewis acids: they draw electron density toward themselves within the PQQ-containing catalytic site. Comparative enzyme experiments and calculations connect metal size and coordination to activity. This provides a chemical rationale for metal preference; the detailed reaction pathway and performance of each enzyme require their own tests. [2]
  • Lanmodulin illustrates selective recognition: a bacterial protein can respond to trivalent lanthanides at concentrations far below those needed for a calcium response. Binding selectivity is an important upstream property, but it is distinct from the enzyme activity that eventually supports metabolism. [3]
  • Mutant and uptake studies in Methylorubrum extorquens AM1 implicated a TonB–ABC transport system in lanthanide incorporation into the cytoplasm. Microscopy showed intracellular metal-rich inclusions resembling polyphosphate granules, adding storage to the acquisition pathway. [4]

The broader picture

Lanthanum can occupy an enzyme’s catalytic site rather than merely stick to a bacterium. In ExaF, La(III) works with the organic cofactor PQQ in alcohol oxidation; the lanthanum-containing enzyme efficiently processed ethanol, whereas the preparation obtained with calcium alone had little activity. Acquiring this metal can therefore unlock useful substrate chemistry for a bacterium. [1]

Experiments worth knowing

Methylobacterium extorquens AM1 and purified ExaF

An alcohol-oxidation role beyond methanol

Study design. Mutant growth experiments, metal analysis, and enzyme assays with calcium-only or calcium-plus-lanthanum cultures.

What they found. Lanthanum-containing ExaF showed substantial alcohol-oxidizing activity. [1]

How to read it. The work identifies a particular enzyme–metal partnership while allowing redundancy with other cellular pathways.

Methylorubrum extorquens AM1 mutants

Following lanthanides into the cell

Study design. Transport-gene deletions, uptake measurements, reporter assays, and electron microscopy.

What they found. Transport components affected growth and intracellular metal handling; cells also formed storage inclusions. [4]

How to read it. Lanthanide use is a regulated cellular process, not simply passive metal adsorption.

Community & host evidence

The cited work concerns cultured methylotrophic bacteria and purified proteins. It does not establish the prevalence of lanthanum-dependent activity in human gut communities. Even within a cultured organism, alternative enzymes can make a pathway useful without making one enzyme absolutely necessary for growth. [1]

Exposure & chemical form

Soluble La(III) added to defined medium is readily accessible compared with many environmental mineral sources. Uptake regulation and intracellular sequestration help explain why supplying a metal and detecting total cell-associated metal are not the same as measuring delivery to the active enzyme. [4]

What the evidence does not establish

ExaF deletion did not abolish ethanol growth in the original study, despite its biochemical contribution to ethanol metabolism. That distinction matters: activity in an enzyme assay, contribution in a cell, and essentiality of an entire organism are progressively stronger claims. [1]

Open research questions

Questions suggested by the evidence and its limits.

  • How is stored lanthanum transferred into the enzyme cofactor, and which parts of the route differ among bacteria? [4]
  • When does selective binding improve access to lanthanum rather than sequester it away from a functional enzyme? [3][4]
