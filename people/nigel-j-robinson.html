Metalloproteins & Metal Homeostasis

Nigel J. Robinson

Made available metal quantitatively distinguishable from total metal

The contribution

What this work established

Robinson and colleagues used bacterial metal sensors to define the intracellular availability of different elements in thermodynamic terms. Comparing those values with a protein’s metal affinities explained how a cobalt-requiring enzyme can receive cobalt even when other metals bind more tightly in isolated biochemical measurements.

  • Osman and colleagues, Bacterial sensors and correct enzyme metalation (2019) ↗

Why this matters to microbial metallomics

This supplies a quantitative foundation for the atlas’s central distinction: a metal can be present without being available to a particular protein. It also makes mismetallation testable. The relevant comparison is between competing intracellular metal pools and protein affinities, rather than between dietary dose and a presumed biological effect.

The next scientific question

A major frontier is extending calibrated measurements of metal availability across species and physiological states, then testing whether they predict protein occupancy in complex host-associated communities.

Research background

Leading theorist of protein "metalation" — how cells ensure each metalloprotein gets the correct metal — and of metal-sensing regulators such as the nickel sensor InrS.
