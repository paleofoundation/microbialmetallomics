Host–Pathogen & Nutritional Immunity

Stéphane L. Benoit

Turning bacterial nickel dependence into an antimicrobial target.

The contribution

What this work established

Benoit and colleagues showed that dimethylglyoxime (DMG), a nickel chelator, inhibited growth of tested multidrug-resistant Salmonella Typhimurium and Klebsiella pneumoniae strains, with protective effects in mouse and insect infection models. A separate Campylobacter jejuni study showed that metal interactions can change the antimicrobial response. These are experimental demonstrations that access to an enzyme cofactor can be a therapeutic research target.

  • Benoit et al. — Nickel chelation against multidrug-resistant enteric pathogens (2019) ↗
  • Benoit & Maier — DMG and copper toxicity in Campylobacter jejuni (2022) ↗

Why this matters to microbial metallomics

His work makes microbial metallomics actionable: a pathogen’s need for a metal can reveal a vulnerability that conventional antibiotic resistance does not describe. It opens an antimicrobial research direction based on metal dependence and enzyme function, connecting basic nickel biology with the search for treatments against resistant infections.

DMG shows why the mechanism deserves attention

What the DMG studies establish
Study settingObserved resultWhy it matters
Tested enteric bacterial strains · laboratory studiesDMG was bacteriostatic against tested Salmonella Typhimurium and Klebsiella pneumoniae, including multidrug-resistant strains. Nickel-dependent enzyme activities were inhibited.Growth inhibition supplies a proof of concept for targeting metal dependence. Bacteriostasis is distinct from killing or eliminating an infection.
Mouse and insect infection models · 2019The study reported improved survival in the tested infection models; the mouse experiment also found reduced bacterial burdens in sampled organs.A biological benefit extended beyond a culture dish. It remains preclinical evidence, with different host systems and intervention timings.
Campylobacter jejuni · laboratory study, 2022DMG inhibited growth. Its combination with copper was bactericidal under the tested conditions; the combined effect did not generalize to selected other Gram-negative bacteria.The surrounding metal environment and the organism matter. Results cannot be extrapolated across all pathogens from nickel dependence alone.

Why this belongs in the antimicrobial-resistance conversation

WHO’s 2024 priority list guides research and investment around defined pathogen–resistance combinations. Benoit’s work is relevant to that agenda because it tests a distinct biological vulnerability in resistant organisms. It is not evidence that DMG controls most WHO priority pathogens, and inclusion here does not imply WHO endorsement. Establishing that breadth would require evidence across the relevant organisms and resistance phenotypes, followed by clinical validation.

  • WHO — Bacterial Priority Pathogens List, 2024 ↗

The next scientific question

Can nickel-directed interventions achieve useful exposure and selectivity in humans while preserving host function and beneficial microbes? The published model results justify that investigation; they do not yet establish a clinical treatment.

Research background

Investigates nickel acquisition, storage and nickel-dependent enzymes in bacterial pathogens. His work on Helicobacter and Campylobacter connects hydrogenase and urease biology with host colonization, respiratory metabolism and iron–sulfur protein maturation. He has also explored nickel chelation against multidrug-resistant enteric bacteria in laboratory and animal-model studies.
