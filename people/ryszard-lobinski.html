Analytical Metallomics & Methods

Ryszard Łobiński

Turning an elemental signal into an identified biological compound

CNRS Research Director, IPREM · CNRS / University of Pau · Pau, France

The contribution

What this work established

Łobiński has developed analytical approaches that combine element-specific detection with molecular mass spectrometry. Work identifying previously unknown selenium metabolites in enriched yeast illustrates the contribution: finding selenium in a sample becomes a starting point for determining which molecules contain it and how microbial metabolism has transformed it.

  • Dernovics, Far and Łobiński — Identification of anionic selenium species in selenium-rich yeast ↗

Why this matters to microbial metallomics

This gives microbial metallomics a way to test claims about chemical form. It is especially relevant when foods or supplements contain products of microbial metabolism: the same total amount of an element can be distributed among different compounds that require separate identification and biological evaluation.

The next scientific question

The frontier is to improve identification and quantification across the full sample while retaining evidence about which compounds survive digestion, enter tissues or remain accessible to microbes.

Research background

Develops analytical approaches for detecting, identifying and quantifying trace-metal species in biological and environmental systems. His work helped establish metallomics and links mass spectrometry, chemical speciation and systems microbiology.

The contribution is grounded in the linked sources. Its significance and future directions are this guide’s editorial synthesis. Inclusion does not imply endorsement of this website.
