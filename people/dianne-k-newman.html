Environmental, Geomicrobiology & Bioremediation

Dianne K. Newman

Connecting mineral chemistry to the physiology of biofilms

The contribution

What this work established

Newman’s work connects microbial electron transfer with both environmental mineral transformations and chronic-infection biofilms. Her group showed that a redox-active phenazine can alter iron availability and support biofilm development in laboratory models, linking the chemical state of iron to collective microbial behavior.

  • Wang et al. — Phenazine, ferrous iron acquisition and biofilm development ↗
  • Caltech — Newman research profile ↗

Why this matters to microbial metallomics

This brings a geobiological insight into medicine: microbes can modify the chemistry that governs their own access to resources. A contaminated environment or infected tissue is therefore an active reaction system, and its oxygen conditions and microbial metabolites can matter alongside total metal abundance.

The next scientific question

Translation requires establishing which redox processes are active in patient biofilms and whether changing those processes improves outcomes while protecting host tissue and microbial ecology.

Research background

Studies bioenergetics and the cell biology of metabolically diverse bacteria. Her work links electron transfer and redox-active metabolites with microbial communities, including biofilms in chronic infections and crop rhizospheres.
