Metalloproteins & Metal Homeostasis

Kevin J. Waldron

Showed why metal deprivation can meet metabolic flexibility

The contribution

What this work established

Waldron and collaborators demonstrated that a Staphylococcus aureus antioxidant enzyme can function with either iron or manganese. Their biochemical and infection-model evidence connected this cofactor flexibility to survival during host-imposed manganese restriction. The work explains an organism’s response to nutritional immunity at the level of enzyme function.

  • Garcia and colleagues, Cofactor flexibility and nutritional immunity (2017) ↗
  • IBB PAS, Laboratory of Metalloprotein Biology ↗

Why this matters to microbial metallomics

This is important for microbiome medicine because an organism’s metal requirement may be conditional. Lowering one metal does not necessarily remove the function that depends on it under ordinary conditions. Waldron’s work makes metabolic flexibility part of evaluating metal-directed interventions and interpreting microbial responses to an inflamed environment.

The next scientific question

A next step is establishing how widespread functional cofactor flexibility is in host-associated communities, and when it changes responses to metal deprivation in clinically relevant settings.

Research background

Studies how metalloproteins acquire the correct metal and how metal specificity evolves. Current laboratory work includes superoxide dismutases, bacterial metal homeostasis and the consequences of metal starvation for enzyme function.
