The field guide

Five questions worth measuring

Proposed research directions, with concrete measurements and the uncertainties each could address.

Start hereEvidence guideOpen questionsEditorial standardsAboutContribute

These are proposed research directions drawn from gaps discussed in the field guide. They are not active studies, funded projects, recruitment notices, or established findings. Each brief identifies what new evidence could clarify.

Question 01 · Exposure and chemical form

When does an elemental total miss the biologically relevant exposure?

The tampon-metals commentary asks whether systemic exposure estimates answer the separate question of local microbial effects. That gap requires direct measurement, not an assumption that an effect occurs.

Measurements to compare
Total elemental content, released chemical species in a justified biological matrix, exposure over time, and microbial or host-response measurements relevant to that setting.
What would clarify the question
A documented link—or lack of one—between the released fraction and a measured response, with appropriate unexposed and matrix controls.
What it would not establish alone
A product-wide safety judgment, a clinical disease risk, or a mechanism from total concentration alone.

Question 02 · Context and reproducibility

Which conditions explain different microbial responses to iron?

Existing reviews contrast infant fortification in Kenya and short-term supplementation in Australian women. The populations and interventions differ, so the comparison generates questions rather than a pooled verdict.

Measurements to compare
Baseline iron status, diet, formulation, duration, baseline microbiome, absolute and relative microbial abundance, inflammation markers, and reported adverse outcomes.
What would clarify the question
A harmonized evidence table showing variables actually available across studies, followed by a prospectively specified test of plausible effect modifiers.
What it would not establish alone
A universal supplement recommendation or an explanation for differences that were not directly tested.

Question 03 · Temporal order

Does a proposed microbial mediator change before the host outcome?

The prenatal-exposure literature connects measurements from different stages of life. Repeated measurements could help distinguish a possible pathway from correlated observations.

Measurements to compare
Metal exposure markers, microbiome composition and function, and the same host outcome at prespecified times, alongside diet, medication, and relevant covariates.
What would clarify the question
Whether exposure precedes microbial change and whether that change precedes the outcome; sensitivity analyses for confounding and missing measurements.
What it would not establish alone
Proof of mediation from a statistical model, or a causal mechanism from one cohort.

Read the cohort review · Midya et al. primary paper ↗ · Inference guide

Question 04 · Location and function

Can spatial metal patterns explain more than a bulk average?

A colorectal-tumor proof-of-concept study pairs elemental imaging and spatial transcriptomics. Replication and matched comparisons are the next questions for a pattern observed in tissue.

Measurements to compare
Elemental maps, tissue features, spatial gene expression, registration uncertainty, and biological replicates; chemical speciation where the question requires it.
What would clarify the question
A reproducible association across independent samples, followed by a separate test of the proposed functional relationship.
What it would not establish alone
That a metal caused a gene-expression program, or that a spatial association supplies a diagnostic test.

Read the study review · Srivastava et al. primary paper ↗ · Speciation reading

Question 05 · Source attribution

How much dietary metal can be traced through the soil–crop pathway?

The fertilizer-history preprint proposes a long-term source contribution. Testing it calls for connected records along the pathway and comparison with alternative inputs.

Measurements to compare
Documented fertilizer composition and application history, soil properties and metal concentrations, crop uptake, food-processing effects, and other plausible sources in the same setting.
What would clarify the question
A transparent mass-balance or source-attribution analysis with uncertainty ranges, missing-data declarations, and independent validation.
What it would not establish alone
Individual dietary exposure, a universal source fraction, or a downstream microbiome effect.
