Follow the metal.

Choose a case. Trace the compartments. Find the connection that still needs evidence.

These diagrams organize findings from distinct studies. Arrows describe a proposed route through the question; they do not mean that every step has been demonstrated in one experiment. There are no calculated doses or predicted patient outcomes.

Human absorption + separate microbial models

Does more zinc reach the host—or less reach the microbes?

The tempting inference is that better host absorption leaves less zinc for microbes. That may change total residual exposure, but chemical accessibility, timing, and location still need measurement. “Aspartate bypasses microbes” remains an untested explanation in the evidence assembled here.

Cellular mechanism + human intervention evidence

The dose enters the gut. Does usable iron reach the marrow?

First distinguish depleted stores, inflammatory restriction, and mixtures. Even in inflammatory bowel disease, depleted stores can accompany low hepcidin. Inflammation does not guarantee a closed export gate in every patient. [7]

Distinct cellular, chemical, and human-specimen studies

A metal delivered as a defense can meet a microbial buffer.

The missing connection is simultaneous measurement of host delivery, ligand-bound versus accessible copper, and the host outcome in the same setting. A total copper result cannot supply all three. These studies do not establish that changing dietary copper would reproduce either mechanism.

Gut bacterial mechanisms + a mouse supplementation study

The cobalt atom does not tell you who can use the molecule.

The transporter studies have not shown that transporter selectivity caused the separate supplementation-associated community shift. Nor do they establish whether a particular community’s corrinoid handling limits host benefit in a defined clinical setting. Free cobalt supplementation is not an equivalent intervention.
